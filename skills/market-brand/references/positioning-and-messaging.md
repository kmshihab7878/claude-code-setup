# Positioning, Messaging, and Vocabulary

Vocabulary categorization (Step 5), messaging hierarchy (Step 8), voice
documentation (Step 9), and copy-sample templates (Step 10). Pair with
`references/brand-workflow.md`.

## Vocabulary Categories

Identify 15-20 most characteristic words/phrases per brand.

### Action words (verbs)

Examples by archetype:

- Authority: "analyze", "validate", "demonstrate", "advise".
- Innovator: "build", "transform", "reimagine", "ship".
- Friend: "help", "support", "make", "share".
- Rebel: "fight", "break", "challenge", "disrupt".
- Guide: "learn", "master", "navigate", "show".

### Descriptive words (adjectives)

- e.g., "powerful", "simple", "enterprise-grade", "effortless".

### Value words

Reflect the brand's stated values.

- e.g., "transparent", "sustainable", "inclusive", "innovative".

### Industry-specific terms

The expected jargon for the category — use selectively based on the
Technical↔Simple dimension score.

- e.g., "workflow", "pipeline", "conversion", "engagement".

### Words to avoid

- Words too casual for the brand (if formal).
- Words too technical for the brand (if simple).
- Competitor terminology deliberately avoided.
- Industry clichés sidestepped (e.g., "synergy", "best-in-class" when the
  brand is the Friend archetype).

### Signature phrases

- **Tagline** — if present.
- **Recurring phrases** — multi-page repetitions.
- **Linguistic patterns** — e.g., always starts sentences with verbs, uses
  dashes frequently, favors short paragraphs.

## Messaging Hierarchy

Document messaging from most distilled to most expanded.

| Layer | Length | Purpose |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Tagline | < 10 words | Most compressed brand message |
| Value Propositions | 1 sentence each (3-5 total) | What we do for whom |
| Elevator Pitch | ~75 words / 30 seconds | Spoken introduction |
| Boilerplate | 100-150 words | Standard "about us" paragraph |
| Full Brand Story | 300-500 words | Complete narrative |

For each layer: status (Exists / Partial / Missing) + improvement
recommendation. If missing, recommend creation; do not fabricate.

## Voice Chart Template

```
OUR VOICE IS:                    OUR VOICE IS NOT:
--------------------------------------------------
[Characteristic 1]              [Anti-characteristic 1]
e.g., "Confident"               e.g., "Arrogant"

[Characteristic 2]              [Anti-characteristic 2]
e.g., "Helpful"                 e.g., "Condescending"

[Characteristic 3]              [Anti-characteristic 3]
e.g., "Clear"                   e.g., "Dumbed down"

[Characteristic 4]              [Anti-characteristic 4]
e.g., "Bold"                    e.g., "Aggressive"
```

The IS / IS NOT contrast is the most valuable single artifact for a new writer
joining the team — it makes the voice operational.

## Writing Do's and Don'ts

### Do

- Use contractions to sound natural (we're, you'll, it's) — when casual score ≥ 6.
- Lead with the benefit, not the feature.
- Use active voice in all headlines and CTAs.
- Address the reader directly with "you" and "your".
- Quote 3-5 specific examples from real source material in every claim.

### Don't

- Use jargon without explaining — when Simple score ≥ 6.
- Use passive voice in calls-to-action.
- Use exclamation marks more than once per paragraph (unless Playful ≥ 8).
- Start sentences with "We" repeatedly — focus on the customer.
- Recommend a tagline or boilerplate when the source has none — flag for creation.

## Differentiation Phrasing

When the consistency audit reveals an opportunity for vocal differentiation,
build the recommendation around concrete phrasing changes — not abstract
guidance. "Switch homepage CTA from `Get Started` to `Try the demo`" beats
"make the CTA more action-oriented."
