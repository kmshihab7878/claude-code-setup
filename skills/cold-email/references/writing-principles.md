# Cold Email Writing Principles

Purpose: Keep the detailed writing rules, examples, voice calibration, subject line guidance, and anti-pattern catalog outside the operating `SKILL.md`.

## Write Like a Peer

The moment an email sounds like marketing copy, reply rates fall. Write like a smart colleague at another company who has a specific reason to start a conversation.

Test every draft with this question: would a friend send this to another friend in business? If not, rewrite it.

Avoid:

```text
I'm reaching out because our platform helps companies like yours achieve unprecedented growth...
```

Prefer:

```text
Noticed you're scaling your SDR team. Timing question: are you doing outbound email in-house or using an agency?
```

## Every Sentence Earns Its Place

Cold email is the wrong place to be thorough. Every sentence should do one of these jobs:

- Create curiosity.
- Establish relevance.
- Build credibility.
- Drive to the ask.

If a sentence does none of those jobs, cut it. Read the draft aloud and remove anything that sounds like droning.

## Personalization Must Connect to the Problem

Generic personalization is worse than none. A school, hobby, or personal detail that does not connect to the business problem feels fake.

Weak personalization:

```text
I saw you went to MIT...
```

Problem-linked personalization:

```text
I saw you're hiring three SDRs, usually a signal that you're trying to scale cold outreach. That's the challenge we help with.
```

## Lead With Their World

The opener should be about the prospect's context, problem, or trigger.

Avoid:

```text
We're a sales intelligence platform that...
```

Prefer:

```text
Your recent product launch mentioned you're entering the SMB market. That shift is hard to run with an enterprise-built playbook.
```

## One Ask Per Email

Do not ask the prospect to book a call, watch a demo, read a case study, and reply with timeline details in the same email. Pick one ask. The more asks you include, the less likely any of them happen.

## Voice Calibration by Audience

| Audience | Length | Tone | Subject Line Style | What Works |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| C-suite | 3-4 sentences | Brief, peer-level, strategic | Short, vague, internal-looking | Big problem, relevant proof, one question |
| VP / Director | 5-7 sentences | Direct, metrics-conscious | Slightly more specific | Specific observation and business angle |
| Mid-level | 7-10 sentences | Practical, shows research | More descriptive | Specific problem, practical value, easy CTA |
| Technical | 7-10 sentences | Precise, no fluff | Technical specificity | Exact problem, precise solution, low-friction ask |

The higher up the org chart, the shorter the email should be. A CEO receives many emails; three sentences and a clear question is useful.

## Subject Lines

Subject lines should get opened. They do not need to convey the full value proposition. The best cold subject lines often look like short internal emails.

### What Works

| Pattern | Example | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| Two or three words | `quick question` | Looks like a note from a colleague |
| Specific trigger + question | `your TechCrunch piece` | Specific without sounding like an ad |
| Shared context | `re: Series B` | Feels relevant and current |
| Observation | `your ATS setup` | Specific and not salesy |
| Referral hook | `[mutual name] suggested I reach out` | Social proof appears early |

### What Kills Opens

- ALL CAPS.
- Emojis in subject lines.
- Fake `Re:` or `Fwd:`.
- Ad-style questions such as "Are you struggling with X?"
- Sender company names in the subject line.
- Blog-headline numbers such as "5 ways to improve..."

## Patterns to Avoid

| Avoid | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| "I hope this email finds you well" | Signals a template immediately |
| "I wanted to reach out because..." | Delays the point |
| Feature dump in email 1 | Features do not matter before trust |
| HTML templates with logos and colors | Looks like marketing and can hurt deliverability |
| Fake `Re:` / `Fwd:` subject lines | Feels deceptive |
| "Just checking in" follow-ups | Adds no value |
| "My name is X and I work at Y" opener | The sender line already says that |
| Unconnected social proof | Proof only matters when tied to the prospect's problem |
| Long case study in email 1 | Save it for later touches |
| Passive CTAs | Use a direct question or specific next step |
