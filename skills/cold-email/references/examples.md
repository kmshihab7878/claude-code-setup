# Cold Email Examples and Output Patterns

Purpose: Keep expanded examples and deliverable shapes available without loading them for every invocation.

## First-Touch Output Pattern

```text
Subject options:
1. quick question
2. your SDR hiring
3. outbound capacity

Email:
Hi [Name],

Noticed [specific trigger connected to the problem].

Teams at this stage often run into [problem] because [reason].

[Credible proof or relevant observation].

Worth comparing notes for 15 minutes next week?
```

Return the email plus a short rationale for the opener, proof point, and ask.

## Sequence Output Pattern

For a full sequence, include:

- Email number and send day.
- Subject line.
- Email body.
- Angle summary.
- Validation note if assumptions or placeholders remain.

Typical sequence:

1. First-touch trigger email.
2. Proof or benchmark follow-up.
3. Alternate pain point.
4. Relevant insight or teardown.
5. Direct question.
6. Breakup email.

## Critique Output Pattern

For draft review:

- Bottom-line diagnosis.
- Line-level issues.
- Rewrite.
- Why the rewrite works.
- Remaining assumptions or missing inputs.

## Performance Diagnosis Pattern

Use available metrics:

- Low opens: subject line, sender reputation, audience fit, timing, or deliverability.
- Opens but low replies: opener, relevance, proof, length, or CTA.
- Replies but wrong outcome: ask, qualification, audience targeting, or offer framing.
- High bounces: list quality, verification, domain age, or send volume.

Recommend one test at a time so results are interpretable.

## Communication Standard

All output should be:

- Bottom line first.
- What + why + how for every major finding.
- Actionable, with owners or deadlines when the user asks for an operating plan.
- Clear about confidence: verified, medium-confidence, or assumed.
