# Cold Email Follow-Up Playbook

Purpose: Keep the detailed follow-up cadence, angle rotation, and breakup pattern available on demand.

## Cadence

Most deals happen in follow-ups, but most follow-ups are weak because they repeat the same ask. Use increasing gaps so outreach is persistent without feeling constant.

| Email | Send Day | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|
| Email 1 | Day 1 | - |
| Email 2 | Day 4 | +3 days |
| Email 3 | Day 9 | +5 days |
| Email 4 | Day 16 | +7 days |
| Email 5 | Day 25 | +9 days |
| Breakup | Day 35 | +10 days |

## Follow-Up Angle Rotation

Each follow-up must have a new angle. Rotate through:

- New evidence: case study, data point, or real customer result.
- New problem angle: a different pain point in the prospect's world.
- Relevant insight: something noticed about their industry, tech stack, hiring, launch, or market move.
- Direct question: a plain question that tests interest or fit.
- Reverse ask: ask who owns the problem if the recipient is not the right person.

## Rules

- Never write "just checking in."
- Do not summarize every previous email.
- Do not make the prospect scroll to understand the current message.
- Keep each follow-up standalone.
- Keep the CTA low-friction.
- Use a professional close-out message at the end of the sequence.

## Breakup Email Pattern

The final email should close the loop. It signals that outreach is ending, which can increase replies because people dislike loose ends.

```text
I'll stop cluttering your inbox after this one.

If [problem] becomes a priority, happy to reconnect. Just reply here and I'll pick it up.

If someone else at [Company] owns this, a name would go a long way.

Either way, good luck with [relevant initiative].
```

Use the breakup only after prior touches added distinct value.
