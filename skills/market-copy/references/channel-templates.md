# Channel Templates

Purpose: page and channel structures for writing copy after the operating contract selects the page type and conversion goal.

## Homepage

1. Hero: headline with what the product does and for whom.
2. Subheadline: how it works or why it matters.
3. Primary CTA.
4. Social proof bar: logos, user count, or key metric when verified.
5. Problem section.
6. Solution section with three key benefits.
7. How it works: three-step process or visual walkthrough.
8. Features and benefits.
9. Testimonials or proof.
10. Final CTA.

## Landing Page

1. Headline: one clear promise.
2. Subheadline: context or evidence.
3. Hero CTA.
4. Problem: short pain articulation.
5. Solution: how the offer fixes the problem.
6. Benefits: outcome-oriented bullets.
7. Social proof.
8. Objection handling or FAQ.
9. Final CTA.

## Pricing Page

1. Headline that frames the investment.
2. Plan names based on audience or use case.
3. Recommended plan, if supported by product strategy.
4. Benefit-oriented feature descriptions.
5. Anchoring through monthly or annual framing.
6. FAQ for pricing objections.
7. Real guarantee, free trial, or cancellation language only if true.

## About Page

1. Mission statement.
2. Origin story from problem to solution.
3. Values with concrete examples.
4. Team credibility and approachability.
5. Social proof or milestones.
6. CTA that connects mission to reader action.

## Product Page

1. Descriptive product title.
2. Price and savings, if true.
3. One-sentence value proposition.
4. Benefit-led description.
5. Specifications in a scannable format.
6. Reviews or proof.
7. Cross-sells, if relevant.

## Feature Page

1. Clear feature name.
2. Problem it solves.
3. How it works.
4. Use cases.
5. Comparison against alternatives.
6. CTA to try, view, or request the feature.

## Blog Post

1. Headline with useful promise.
2. Intro that names the reader's problem.
3. Clear section headings.
4. Examples or proof.
5. Contextual CTA after the helpful content.
6. Meta title and description.

## Contact Or Demo Page

1. Form headline that states what happens next.
2. Friction-reducing body copy.
3. Proof or trust signals near the form.
4. Field labels that are clear and minimal.
5. CTA matching the action: demo, quote, audit, or consultation.

## Email And Ad Copy

- Subject line or hook: one idea, concrete benefit.
- Opening line: reader pain, desire, or trigger event.
- Body: proof, mechanism, and objection handling.
- CTA: one action.
- Compliance: unsubscribe and required disclaimers where applicable.
